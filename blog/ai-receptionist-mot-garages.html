AI Receptionists for Garages: Bookings Without the Phone Tag

15 November 2026 · Scale Up Softwares

It is half past four on a Friday. You are underneath a car, halfway through a service, with oil on your hands and a torque wrench in one of them. The phone rings in the office. It rings again. By the time you have wiped your hands and got to it, the caller has gone — and the voicemail light is blinking.

That caller wanted an MOT booked for next week. They will ring the next garage on the list, and the one after that, until somebody answers. This post explains what an AI receptionist for garages does, which garage calls it handles well, and where a person still needs to pick up.

What customers actually ring a garage about

Most calls to a garage fall into a small number of groups. Bookings are the biggest: an MOT, a service, a repair, a tyre change. Then come the questions — how much is an MOT, how long will a service take, do you have a courtesy car, can you look at a noise the car started making yesterday. Then the follow-ups: is my car ready, what did you find, how much is it going to cost.

And then there are the calls that need a person straight away: a breakdown, a car that will not start, a warning light on the motorway, an upset customer disputing a bill. The first three groups are routine. They follow a pattern, they need the same few details, and they can be handled well by an AI receptionist that has been set up with your garage's prices, diary and working hours. The last group is different, and any honest account of AI in a garage has to say so clearly.

How an AI receptionist answers a garage call

An AI voice agent answers the phone the way a careful member of your team would. It introduces itself honestly as a virtual assistant, asks what the call is about, and then follows the steps you have agreed for that kind of call. It works from your garage's own information: your MOT and service prices, your opening hours, which jobs need a ramp, and how long each job takes.

For a booking, it asks for the registration number, the work needed and a day that suits, then offers the slots that are actually free and confirms the booking on the call. It sends your team a written summary of every call by email, so nobody has to take notes while the phone is ringing. For a routine question, it answers from the information you have given it. For anything it cannot answer — a strange noise, a warning light, a price for a job it has not been told about — it takes the details and arranges a call-back from a technician.

Because the agent can answer more than one call at a time, the Friday afternoon rush stops being a queue. Customers get through on the first ring, and your mechanics can stay under the cars instead of drying their hands to answer the phone. MOT reminders and service bookings

An MOT runs out once a year, and most drivers only remember when the reminder letter arrives — or when they realise it expired last week. A garage that reminds its customers is a garage that keeps its diary full, and it does not take much: a call or a message a few weeks before the MOT is due, offering a slot.

An AI voice agent can work through that list for you. It rings customers whose MOT is coming up, offers a date, books the slot and confirms it on the call. A WhatsApp booking agent can do the same in a message, for customers who prefer to tap than to talk — and it can send a reminder the day before, so fewer customers forget and leave a ramp empty. Every missed MOT booking is a job that went to the garage that rang first, and our guide to what one missed call really costs a UK business looks at the other side of the same issue: the customer, the diary slot and the hours spent chasing it.

The same approach works for servicing. A customer who had a service last year is due another one this year. A reminder call, a booked slot and a confirmation — that is three small jobs an agent can do while your team gets on with the work in the workshop. For example, imagine a garage that services two hundred cars a year: a reminder for each one, made at the right time, is the difference between a diary that fills itself and a diary with gaps in it.

What it cannot do

An AI receptionist cannot diagnose a fault. A customer describing a noise, a smell or a warning light needs a technician, not a script — and the agent is set up to say so and pass the call on, not to guess. It cannot quote a repair it has not seen, either. It can give your standard prices for MOTs and fixed-price services, and for anything else it takes the details and asks a technician to ring back with a quote.

It cannot handle a breakdown. A driver stranded at the roadside needs a person straight away, and the agent passes those calls to your team immediately, or takes the driver's location and number and arranges an urgent call-back. It never gives safety advice about driving a car with a fault.

It also cannot replace the judgement of your team. A customer disputing a bill, a regular who expects a favour, a job that has gone wrong — those conversations need a person. What the agent does is clear the routine calls out of the way, so your team has time for the conversations that matter. Calls involve personal data, and we agree a data processing agreement with each client, explain where data is stored and how long it is kept, and use data only to run their agent. Common questions

Will customers know they are talking to an AI?
Yes. The agent introduces itself honestly as a virtual assistant at the start of the call. Customers can ask for a person or a call-back at any point, and anything complicated is passed to your team.

Can it book MOTs and services into our diary?
Yes. It offers the slots that are actually free for the work the customer needs, confirms the booking on the call, and sends a written summary to your team. If a job needs a ramp or a specialist, it books the right kind of slot.

Can it give a price for a repair over the phone?
No, and it should not try. It can quote your standard prices for MOTs and fixed-price services, and for anything else it takes the details and asks a technician to ring the customer back with a quote.

What happens if a customer breaks down?
The agent passes breakdown calls to your team straight away, or takes the customer's location and number and arranges an urgent call-back. It never tries to diagnose a fault or give safety advice over the phone.
