AI Receptionists for Property Managers: Every Tenant Call Answered

21 November 2026 · Scale Up Softwares

It is 8:47 on a Monday morning. Your phone is already ringing: a tenant reporting a leak, a landlord asking about a void period, and a contractor who needs the key safe code. By the time you have dealt with the first two, the third has gone to voicemail — and the tenant with the leak has rung twice more.

That is the problem an AI receptionist for property managers is built for. This post explains the calls a property management office actually gets, what an AI voice agent does with each kind, and how it sorts emergencies from routine repairs.

The calls a property manager actually gets

A property management phone line is not one kind of call. It is several, all arriving at once, and each needs a different response.

Tenant repairs. The largest group by volume: a dripping tap, a broken oven, a window that will not close, a boiler that has stopped. The tenant wants the problem logged and a visit arranged. A repair call handled well takes a few minutes; a voicemail nobody returns becomes a second call, then a complaint.

Emergencies. A burst pipe, water coming through a ceiling, no heating in winter, a front door that will not lock. Rarer, but they cannot wait for office hours — an emergency reported at 11pm still needs a response that night.

Rent and tenancy questions. When is the rent due, has a payment gone through, what is the notice period, can a tenant keep a pet. Quick to answer, but they still tie up a phone line.

Viewings and new enquiries. Prospective tenants ringing about a property they saw online, and landlords ringing about having their property managed. These are the calls that bring in money, and they often arrive in the evening — exactly when the office is closed.

Contractors. Plumbers, electricians and cleaners ringing for access codes, keys, or to confirm a job. Short calls, but frequent, and they interrupt everything else.

The pattern is the same in every office: the urgent and the routine arrive together, and the routine is what gets dropped. Our guide to what one missed call really costs a UK business looks at the same problem from the other side.

What an AI receptionist does on a property management call

An AI voice agent answers your phone around the clock and handles each kind of call the way you have set it up to. It introduces itself honestly as a virtual assistant.

For a repair, it takes the details that matter: which property, what the problem is, how long it has been going on, and whether anyone is at home to let a contractor in. It logs the request, so your team starts the morning with a list they can act on rather than a voicemail to decode. For a viewing enquiry, it offers real available slots, confirms the appointment on the call, and sends a text confirmation.

For a rent or tenancy question it can answer from your standard information — due dates, notice periods, your pet policy — it answers on the spot. For anything it cannot answer, it takes a clear message and arranges a call-back. And because the agent answers in the evening as well as during the day, it reaches people when they are free to talk: a tenant who works during your opening hours may never pick up at 11am, but may happily report a repair at 7pm.

For tenants who prefer messaging, a WhatsApp booking agent can take the first report and book the viewing or the contractor visit in the chat — so the report still gets logged instead of waiting for a phone call that never comes.

Repairs and emergencies: sorting the urgent from the routine

The most important job in property management is triage: deciding what needs someone tonight and what can wait until Monday. An AI receptionist does this by following rules you set.

Before the agent goes live, you decide which problems count as emergencies for your portfolio — water coming through a ceiling, no heating in winter, a broken front door, a gas smell, a complete loss of power — and what should happen when one is reported. The agent passes those calls straight to your team or your out-of-hours contractor. Everything else is logged as a routine repair, with the details your contractors need.

Getting triage wrong costs in both directions: an emergency treated as routine becomes a bigger repair and an unhappy tenant, while a routine repair treated as an emergency becomes a call-out fee and a contractor woken for a dripping tap. A clear set of rules, applied the same way on every call, avoids both. If contractors do most of your repair work, our guide for tradespeople covers the other side of the same calls.

Viewings and new landlord enquiries

An AI receptionist books viewings into your diary from the calls your office never hears: evenings, weekends and bank holidays. It asks the questions you would ask — how many people are moving in, when they need to move, whether they have a deposit ready — and passes the answers to your team with the booking. It does the same for landlords: a landlord ringing about having a property managed gets their details taken and a call-back arranged.

Estate agents face the same pattern, and our guide to AI voice agents for estate agents covers viewing requests and valuations in more detail. The difference for a property manager is that the call does not end at the viewing: the same tenant may ring again a month later about a repair.

What it doesn't do

An AI receptionist is a first line, not a replacement for your team.

It cannot inspect a property, judge how serious a repair is from a description alone, or negotiate with a tenant or a landlord. A tenant in dispute about a deposit, a landlord unhappy with a void period, or a neighbour complaint needs a person who can listen, use judgement and take responsibility — so the agent is set up to pass those calls to your team.

It cannot give legal advice — on tenancy law, deposit protection or eviction, for example — and it should never be asked to. If a caller needs legal advice, the right answer is to point them to a qualified adviser. This is general information, not legal advice.

On data protection, we agree a data processing agreement with each client, explain where data is stored and how long it is kept, and use data only to run their agent.

Common questions

Will tenants mind talking to an AI voice agent?
The agent introduces itself honestly as a virtual assistant, and tenants can ask for a person or a call-back at any point. Most tenants care far more about getting their problem logged quickly than about who — or what — answered the phone.

Can the agent tell a real emergency from a routine repair?
Yes, within the rules you set. You decide which problems count as emergencies — water coming through a ceiling, no heating in winter, a broken front door — and the agent passes those straight to your team. Everything else is logged with the details your contractors need.

Can it book viewings into our diary?
Yes. It offers real available viewing slots, confirms the appointment on the call, and sends the caller a text confirmation. You receive a summary of every call.

What happens if a caller asks for a specific person?
The agent takes a message or arranges a call-back from that person. If the matter is urgent and the person is available, it can transfer the call to them.

Do we need to change our phone number?
No. You keep your existing number; we connect the agent to it.
