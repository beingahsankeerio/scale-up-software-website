How Much Does an AI Receptionist Cost in the UK? (2026 Guide)

23 October 2026 · Scale Up Softwares

It's Tuesday evening, just after six. You're locking up the practice when the phone rings — a patient wanting to move Thursday's appointment to Friday. There is nobody at the desk to answer, and by Wednesday morning they have booked with the practice down the road.

Moments like that are why business owners ask: how much does an AI receptionist cost in the UK? An AI receptionist answers every call, takes bookings and handles the routine questions your team never has time for. This guide explains how AI receptionist pricing usually works, what our plans cost, what drives the price up or down, and how to work out whether it is worth it for your business.

How AI receptionist pricing usually works

Most AI receptionists in the UK are sold as a monthly subscription. You pay a flat fee each month, and the plan includes a bundle of call minutes. While your agent is on the phone with callers, it uses minutes from the bundle; if you go over, the extra minutes are charged on top.

A "call minute" is exactly what it sounds like: one minute of the agent on a call. Most routine calls — booking, rescheduling, answering a question about opening hours — take a couple of minutes. As a rule of thumb, a 200-minute bundle covers around 70 to 100 calls. That is the basis of our Starter plan.

Setup is sometimes charged separately and sometimes included. With us, setup is included: we build the agent, train it on your business, and connect it to your number and diary. Contracts vary between providers — ours are rolling monthly, so you can cancel any time.

What pushes the price up or down

Three things decide which plan you need — and therefore what you pay.

1. How many calls you get. A quiet week for a small salon and a busy week for a dental practice are different worlds. The best guide is your own call log: most phone systems can show how many calls came in last month and how many went unanswered. Pick a plan with some headroom for your busiest weeks.

2. What the agent needs to do. Simple booking and message-taking sit on the cheaper plans. Text confirmations and reminders, urgent-call transfer to your team, and coverage across multiple locations sit on the bigger ones — because they need more minutes and more setup.

3. How your diary connects. Linking the agent to your booking system is part of our setup, which is included in every plan. If your practice runs on paper diaries, we will work around that in the demo — the point is to see how it fits your business before you spend anything.

If your call volume is tiny, the cheapest plan — or no agent at all — may be the honest answer, and we will tell you so on the demo. An AI receptionist earns its keep where the phone rings when your team can't pick up: mornings, lunchtimes and after closing.

How the cost compares with a human receptionist

The most useful comparison is with the alternative: employing a receptionist. The median gross pay for a full-time receptionist in the UK is £24,678 a year (that's before tax, full-time employees only, and the 2025 figures are provisional).

Against that, our plans work out to £1,188 a year for Starter, £2,388 for Growth and £4,188 for Complete. That is the full cost — no recruitment fees, no sick cover to arrange, no training.

That comparison needs honesty, though. An AI receptionist answers calls. It does not do everything a person at your front desk does — greeting walk-ins, handling payments, keeping the diary moving through the day. The fair way to read the numbers is that an AI receptionist gives you every call answered for a few thousand pounds a year, in the gaps where even the best receptionist is busy with the patient in front of them.

Sources: Office for National Statistics, Annual Survey of Hours and Earnings 2025 (provisional), Table 14.7a, UK.

Things to consider before you sign up

A few honest points, whichever provider you choose.

Minutes are real usage. Your bundle is used when the agent is actually talking to callers, so size the plan for your busy months, not your quiet ones. With us, going over just means 25p a minute — the agent doesn't stop answering.

Urgent and complex calls still need a human. A good setup transfers those calls to you or your team with the caller's details, rather than trying to handle them. Make sure this is agreed when the agent is built, not after.

Ask about data handling. We agree a data processing agreement with each client, explain where data is stored and how long it is kept, and use data only to run your agent. If you run a clinic, patient and health information is special category data under UK GDPR, and we treat it that way. (This is general information, not legal advice.)

Watch the contract. A rolling monthly plan lets you walk away if the agent isn't earning its keep. If a provider asks for a year up front, ask them why they need it.

Common questions

Do I pay per call or per month?
Per month. Each plan includes a bundle of call minutes, and any extra minutes are 25p each. You are not charged per booking or per appointment.

What happens if I go over my minutes?
The agent keeps answering. Extra minutes are billed at 25p each, so an unexpectedly busy month doesn't mean missed calls.

Is there a long contract?
No. Our plans are rolling monthly and you can cancel any time.

Do I keep my phone number?
Yes. The agent connects to your existing number. Every agent introduces itself as a virtual assistant, and callers can ask for a person or a call-back at any point.

Can it book straight into my diary?
Yes — it offers real available slots and confirms the appointment there and then. Urgent calls are transferred to you with the caller's details. If you run a dental practice, our guide to AI receptionists for dental clinics walks through what that looks like on a real call.
