AI Receptionists for Hotels and Guesthouses: Bookings Around the Clock

27 November 2026 · Scale Up Softwares

It is 11:40 on a Thursday night. A couple driving up the motorway want a room for tonight, and they ring your hotel. The reception desk closed at ten, the night porter is dealing with a guest on the second floor, and the phone rings out. The couple ring the next hotel on their list instead — and book there.

That is the everyday problem an AI receptionist for hotels is built for. This guide explains what an AI receptionist does on a hotel's calls — booking enquiries, reservations, guest questions and messages — what it costs, and what it cannot do.

The calls a hotel misses every week

A hotel's phone never keeps office hours. Guests ring late at night to book a room for the same evening, early in the morning to ask about check-in times, and at weekends to plan a stay. Small hotels and guesthouses often have nobody on the desk for much of the day: the owner is cleaning rooms, serving breakfast or showing a guest to their room, and the phone is ringing in an empty reception.

The calls that come in are a familiar mix. People ask whether you have a room free tonight, what the price is for two nights, whether you have a family room, a ground-floor room or parking, and what time breakfast is served. Existing guests ring to change their arrival time, ask for an extra night, request a cot for a small child, or check whether they can bring a dog. None of these calls is difficult. They are simply frequent, and they arrive at the worst moments.

Many callers who reach voicemail never ring back. They book the hotel that answers. A guest who cannot change an arrival time or get a simple question answered starts their stay annoyed — and annoyed guests leave the sharp reviews.

What an AI receptionist does on a hotel call

An AI voice agent answers every call to your hotel, day and night, and introduces itself honestly as a virtual assistant. Callers can ask for a person or a call-back at any point, and the agent passes them on. It is not pretending to be a member of your team.

It answers the routine questions from the information you give it: room types and prices, availability, check-in and check-out times, breakfast hours, parking, whether you take dogs, and how to find you. A caller asking whether you have a double room free on Friday gets an answer straight away, instead of a voicemail message and a wait.

When a caller wants to book, the agent offers real available rooms and confirms the reservation on the call — the dates, the room type, the price and any extras the guest asked for. If the caller has a question it cannot answer, it takes a clear message with the caller's name and number, or passes the call to your team. You receive a summary of every call, so nothing slips through.

Because the agent works outside your opening hours, it catches the calls your team never hears: the driver who decides to stop for the night at 10pm, the couple planning a weekend away on a Sunday evening, the business traveller who rings before breakfast for a room tomorrow. These are often the callers most ready to book, and they are the ones an empty desk misses most.

Bookings, direct bookings and fewer booking-site fees

A hotel booking usually starts with one phone call, and that call often decides where the person stays. The agent answers the price question honestly, explains what is included — breakfast, parking, a sea view — and books the room there and then. A caller who has a confirmed reservation is far more likely to turn up than one who was told someone would ring them back.

There is a second benefit to answering the phone yourself. A booking taken direct, over your own phone, is one you do not pay commission on to a booking site — it stays yours, at the price you set, with the guest's details in your hands.

For guests who are already staying with you, the agent handles the steady stream of requests that otherwise ties up reception: a later check-out, an extra night, a table for dinner, a taxi in the morning. A guest can sort these by phone without waiting at the desk. If your Growth plan includes text confirmations and reminders, the guest gets a confirmation message as well, which cuts down on no-shows.

Some guests prefer messaging to calling. A WhatsApp booking agent can answer the same questions and take the same bookings in a chat, which suits guests who message while they are travelling or comparing hotels. Booking-based businesses of all kinds work the same way: our guide to AI receptionists for restaurants covers the same pattern for table bookings, and AI receptionists for property managers shows how it works for viewings and tenant calls.

One thing worth knowing: the agent only books what is actually available. It works from your rooms, your rates and your rules — so a room that is already booked stays booked, and a rate is only quoted when it is the rate you set for those dates.

What an AI receptionist doesn't do

An AI receptionist cannot show a guest to their room, carry a bag, or sort out a problem with the heating. A guest at the desk with a complaint, a request for a different room, or a question about the area needs a person — the agent's job is to get the caller to the right person or booking, not to replace that welcome.

It cannot handle complaints, refunds or payment disputes either. A guest disputing a charge or asking for a refund needs a human who can look at their booking and make a decision. The agent recognises these calls and passes them to your team, or arranges a call-back, rather than trying to resolve them.

It also cannot deal with an emergency. If a caller reports a fire alarm, a medical problem or anything urgent at the property, the agent passes the call to a person straight away and does not try to manage the situation itself. That boundary matters in a hotel more than in most businesses, so it is set up explicitly when your agent is built.

And it is not a salesperson. It answers questions honestly and books the room, but it does not pressure anyone into booking, and it does not invent offers or discounts. An AI receptionist covers the calls your team cannot get to; it does not replace your team.

Common questions

Will guests mind being answered by an AI voice agent?
The agent introduces itself honestly as a virtual assistant, and guests can ask for a person or a call-back at any point. Most guests care far more about booking a room quickly or getting a straight answer than about who — or what — answered the phone.

Can the agent take bookings and check room availability?
Yes. It offers real available rooms from the availability, rates and rules you give it, and confirms the reservation on the call, and you receive a summary of every call by email.

What happens if a guest has a complaint or an emergency?
The agent is set up to recognise calls that need a human — complaints, payment disputes, emergencies or anything urgent at the property — and pass them to your team straight away, or arrange a call-back.

Does it answer outside our reception hours?
Yes. The agent answers around the clock, including late nights, early mornings, weekends and bank holidays — the times when people often decide to book a room.

Do we need to change our phone number?
No. You keep your existing number; we connect the agent to it.
