AI Receptionists for Tradespeople: Never Miss a Job While You're on One

16 November 2026 · Scale Up Softwares

It is half past ten on a Tuesday. You are under a kitchen sink, spanner in one hand and a torch in the other, and your phone is ringing in the van outside. By the time you have washed your hands and walked out to it, the call has gone to voicemail — and the caller, who needed a plumber today, has already rung the next name on their list.

That is the working day of most tradespeople. An AI receptionist for tradespeople exists for exactly this problem: it answers the calls you cannot get to, takes the details of the job, books a visit into your diary, and tells you about it in a short summary. This guide explains what it does on a call, what it cannot do, and what it costs.

Why tradespeople miss so many calls

Most tradespeople do not miss calls because they are careless. They miss them because the job makes answering impossible. Your hands are full, you are up a ladder, you are under a floor, or you are driving between jobs. A phone call is a two-handed, two-eared task, and most of the working day leaves you with neither to spare.

The other half of the problem is that there is nobody else to answer. A one-person business has no receptionist, and a small firm with two or three vans rarely has anyone sitting by a phone. The work comes first, because it has to — but every call that goes unanswered is a job you never hear about.

And callers in a hurry rarely wait. Someone with a leaking pipe, a broken boiler or a fuse box that has tripped does not want to leave a message and hope. They want a person, or at least an answer, now. If they reach your voicemail, many will simply try the next tradesperson — and the job goes with them. What an AI receptionist does on a tradesperson's call

An AI voice agent answers in your business name and introduces itself honestly as a virtual assistant. It is not pretending to be you, and it does not claim to be human. It says what it is, then gets on with the call.

First, it takes the details that matter: the caller's name and number, what the job is, where the property is, and when they need it done. A dripping tap next week is a different job from a burst pipe this afternoon, and the agent is set up to tell the difference.

Then it acts on what it hears. For a routine job it can offer real available slots and book a visit into your diary, then confirm the time with the caller on the call. For a quote, it takes the details and sends you a summary so you can ring back with a price. For an emergency, it passes the call to you straight away.

It also works when you don't. Most people ring a tradesperson in the evening, after their own working day ends. An AI receptionist answers at 7pm and on a Sunday morning just as it does at 10am, so the call that comes in while you are having dinner still gets answered. If a customer would rather message than call, a WhatsApp booking agent can take the same enquiry in a chat and book it there.

Sorting the jobs worth taking

Not every call is a job you want, and an AI receptionist can help with that too. Before anything reaches your diary, it can ask the questions you would ask: what kind of work is it, where is it, and when does it need doing. A job fifty miles outside your area, or one that needs doing tomorrow when your diary is full for a fortnight, can be handled politely without taking up your evening.

That sorting only works if you set it up well. The agent is trained on your business: the areas you cover, the jobs you do and don't do, your working hours, and the questions you want asked. The clearer your rules, the better the calls it takes.

You stay in control throughout. Every call ends with a short summary sent to you by email, so you can see at a glance who rang, what they wanted, and what was booked. If something was booked wrongly, you can move it. Nothing is hidden, and nothing is decided for you.

What it doesn't do

An AI receptionist cannot price a job it has not seen, and it should never try. A quote for a bathroom refit or a rewiring job needs your eyes on the property, and any agent that invented a price would do your reputation real damage. The honest setup is simple: the agent takes the details and you price the job.

It also cannot do the skilled work, diagnose a fault over the phone beyond the basics, or settle an argument with an unhappy customer. Anything complicated, urgent or personal goes to a human — either you, or someone on your team. An emergency, a complaint, or a customer in distress is not a job for an automated call.

There are practical limits too. The agent needs your diary and your rules kept up to date. If your availability changes and nobody tells it, it will book a visit you cannot make. And like any tool that handles customer details, it needs to be set up with data protection in mind: we agree a data processing agreement with each client, explain where data is stored and how long it is kept, and use data only to run their agent. Common questions

Will customers mind being answered by an AI voice agent?
The agent introduces itself honestly as a virtual assistant, and callers can ask for a person or a call-back at any point. Most customers care far more about getting their job booked quickly than about who — or what — answered the phone.

Can it book jobs into my diary?
Yes. It offers real available slots and confirms the visit on the call, and you receive a summary of every call by email.

What happens if a caller has an emergency, like a burst pipe?
The agent is set up to recognise urgent calls and pass them to you straight away. Routine enquiries are handled on the call; emergencies go to a human.

Can it take messages for quotes?
Yes. It takes the caller's details and a description of the job, then sends you a summary so you can ring back with a price. It never invents a price for a job it has not seen.

Do I need to change my phone number?
No. You keep your existing number; we connect the agent to it.
